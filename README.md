### Introduction
Artificial intelligence (AI) has quickly become part of everyday life in higher 
education, with students using it to study, write, and solve problems. While these
tools can support learning, they are also changing the job market that graduates 
are preparing to enter, raising uncertainty about which careers will remain 
secure. This uncertainty can lead to career anxiety, or worry about future 
employment and professional prospects. Understanding how students' use of AI
relates to these feelings is important for universities that want to prepare their
students for an AI-driven workplace while supporting their well-being. This 
project therefore explores the relationship between the use of AI and career
anxiety among students in higher education.

#### Data Description

The dataset we are using is from Mendeley Data contributed by Islam et al., 2026. 
This dataset contains survey responses from 3,156 students across five Bangladeshi
universities (Daffodil International University, Jahangirnagar University, 
American International University-Bangladesh, University of Dhaka, and Chittagong
University of Engineering and Technology). It captures students' knowledge and use
of AI tools, how much they see AI as a threat to their desired careers, and their
self-reported career anxiety. The data can support research on career anxiety, 
labor market trends, and human-AI interaction.
